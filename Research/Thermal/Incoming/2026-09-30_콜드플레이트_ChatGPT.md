COLD PLATE RESEARCH TRENDS: DETAILED LITERATURE RECONNAISSANCE
2024-2026, with emphasis on electronics / data-center liquid cooling
Prepared: 2026-09-30
Purpose: Research preparation for a conference abstract

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
======================================================================

This document extends the initial cold-plate trend survey into a more
paper-level literature reconnaissance, emphasizing 2024-2026 research
and the transition from conventional microchannel cold plates toward
high-heat-flux, hotspot-aware, topology-optimized, additively
manufactured, package-integrated, and system-level liquid cooling.

The central observation is:

    conventional cold plate
        -> microchannel geometry optimization
        -> manifold / flow-distribution engineering
        -> enhanced fins / pin fins / jets / bionic channels
        -> topology optimization
        -> TPMS / graded 3-D structures
        -> topology + manufacturability
        -> package-integrated / near-chip cooling
        -> two-phase / boiling cooling
        -> transient and non-uniform heat-load optimization
        -> rack / data-center energy and reliability optimization

A second, equally important transition is from optimizing a single
thermal metric to optimizing a system of coupled metrics:

    Tmax, temperature uniformity, thermal resistance,
    pressure drop, pumping power, coolant temperature rise,
    manufacturability, reliability, and sometimes PUE.

In other words, the research question is increasingly changing from

    "How do I increase the heat-transfer coefficient?"

to

    "How do I remove a realistic, non-uniform, very high heat load
     while controlling hydraulic cost, package constraints, and
     reliability?"

For conference-abstract positioning, this distinction is important.
A new channel shape by itself is now a crowded contribution space.
A stronger positioning usually comes from a clearly defined missing
piece in the chain:

    realistic heat map
    + flow-distribution problem
    + design methodology
    + hydraulic constraint
    + manufacturable geometry
    + experimental / system validation.

1. SCOPE AND EVIDENCE NOTE
======================================================================

Search scope:
- Main period: 2024-01-01 through 2026-09-30
- Primary focus: liquid cold plates, microchannel / manifold microchannel
  heat sinks, direct-to-chip cooling, and package-integrated microfluidics
  for high-power electronics and data centers
- Secondary branch: battery cold plates when the work is methodologically
  relevant (non-uniform heat, topology optimization, surrogate modeling)
- Sources prioritized:
    publisher article pages,
    Nature / Science / Elsevier article metadata and abstracts,
    university repositories,
    PubMed / institutional records,
    ASME conference records / conference program.
- This is not a Scopus/WoS export or a fully systematic PRISMA review.
  The purpose is research direction mapping, not a formal bibliometric
  review.
- Numeric performance values below are reported values from the cited
  studies. They should not be compared directly across papers without
  checking footprint area, heat-input definition, coolant, inlet
  temperature, boundary condition, TIM, pressure-drop definition,
  and whether the value is experimental or numerical.

Important terminology:
- "Cold plate" is used broadly in industry.
- "Microchannel heat sink" / "manifold microchannel heat sink" may be
  physically integrated into a package or substrate and are not
  interchangeable with a conventional mechanically attached cold plate.
- "Direct-to-chip" in industry often still includes a cold plate and TIM;
  "direct-to-package" and "direct-to-die" can mean different levels of
  thermal integration.

2. THE CURRENT FIELD IN ONE PAGE
======================================================================

2.1. Why liquid cold plates remain important

High-power CPU/GPU/ASIC systems continue to push heat flux and thermal
design power upward. Reviews in 2025 and 2026 identify cold-plate
liquid cooling and microchannel heat sinks as major solutions because
microchannels provide short conduction paths, high area density, and
high convective capability.

The 2025 Chemical Engineering Science review emphasizes cold-plate
materials, coolant properties, inlet/outlet configurations, microchannel
design, and the need for more internal-flow visualization. It also notes
the continued lack of reliable in-situ flow visualization and prediction
models for some microchannel regimes.

The 2026 Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews article frames
microchannel heat sinks for data-center cold plates around four themes:
materials, manufacturing, biomimetic/topological design, and performance
evaluation. It explicitly discusses the progression from subtractive
manufacturing toward additive manufacturing and stresses multi-objective
performance metrics.

Primary source:
- Zhou, Weinan; Dong, Kaijun. "Research progress of the liquid cold plate
  cooling technology for server electronic chips: A review." International
  Journal of Energy Research, 2022.
  DOI: 10.1002/er.7979

Recent reviews:
- "A comprehensive review of cold plate liquid cooling technology for
  data centers." Chemical Engineering Science, 2025, 121525.
  DOI: 10.1016/j.ces.2025.121525
- "Microchannel heat sinks for cold plate liquid cooling in data centers:
  Advances, evaluations and prospects." Renewable and Sustainable Energy
  Reviews, 2026, 116729.
  DOI: 10.1016/j.rser.2026.116729

2.2. Dominant design trade-off

Almost every cold-plate architecture eventually encounters:

    higher area / smaller channels / stronger mixing
                  -> better heat transfer
                  -> higher pressure loss

Therefore a meaningful engineering objective often looks like:

    minimize Tmax or Rth
    subject to DeltaP <= limit

or

    minimize [Rth, pumping power, temperature non-uniformity]

rather than maximizing h alone.

2.3. Current frontier

The highest-interest research directions from the 2024-2026 literature
are:

(A) Flow distribution and manifolds
(B) High heat flux and hotspot cooling
(C) Topology optimization
(D) Additive / electrochemical manufacturing
(E) TPMS and other 3-D architected structures
(F) Non-uniform / time-dependent heat loading
(G) Two-phase flow boiling
(H) Package-integrated / near-chip cooling
(I) System-level energy and reliability
(J) Reduced-order / surrogate / data-driven optimization

3. RESEARCH AXIS A: MICROCHANNEL GEOMETRY IS STILL ACTIVE,
   BUT THE PROBLEM IS NO LONGER JUST "CHANNEL SIZE"
======================================================================

Traditional variables:
- channel width
- channel depth / height
- aspect ratio
- fin thickness
- pitch
- channel number
- hydraulic diameter
- serpentine curvature
- rib height
- inlet/outlet location

Recent work adds:
- layered channels
- stepped channels
- cross-flow
- variable fin amplitude
- local pin-fin distribution
- graded cell size
- manifold geometry
- multiple inlet / outlet networks.

Key paper:
Li, Yu-Ting et al. "Examining performance of microchannel cold plate heat
sink from its three parts along coolant flow direction and new structure
designs based on the new considerations." Applied Thermal Engineering,
269, 125963, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2025.125963

Key idea:
The cold plate can be decomposed into three linked functions:
    inlet/outlet -> manifold -> microchannel
Rather than treating "channel shape" as the whole cold plate, the paper
constructs new structures by combining design choices for the three
segments. 26 structures were generated and assessed using Taguchi design
and TOPSIS. The paper reports an improved design reaching 266.60 W/cm^2
with q/DeltaP = 14.08 W/(cm^-2 kPa).

Research implication:
A conference abstract can be stronger when it identifies which part of
the flow path is actually limiting performance. A new channel geometry
without a flow-distribution argument is increasingly difficult to
differentiate.

Another paper:
Wang, Han et al. "A study on the effect of channel structures on flow and
heat transfer performance of cold plate with double-layer serpentine
microchannel." Applied Thermal Engineering, 259, 124944, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.124944

Reported finding:
- parallel flow path improves flow distribution;
- rib height had the strongest effect among examined structural factors;
- uniform flow distribution reduced thermal conduction resistance.

======================================================================
4. RESEARCH AXIS B: MANIFOLD AND FLOW DISTRIBUTION
======================================================================

This is one of the most important threads across recent work.

Problem:
Parallel microchannels can suffer flow maldistribution because the
pressure distribution in the manifold is comparable to the channel
pressure drop.

The design logic can be expressed qualitatively as:

    if manifold dynamic-pressure variation is large compared with
    channel pressure loss
        -> channel flow rates become non-uniform
        -> some regions are under-cooled
        -> hotspot risk rises.

Very useful reduced-order paper:
Lee, Hansol; Lee, Young Jin; Kim, Sung Jin.
"One-dimensional model of manifold microchannels for embedded cooling:
Prediction of thermal performance and flow non-uniformity."
International Communications in Heat and Mass Transfer, 155, 107498, 2024.
DOI: 10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2024.107498

The model was tested over broad inlet Reynolds number and geometric
ranges. Reported RMS percentage errors:
- thermal performance: about 6%
- flow non-uniformity: about 26%
The authors propose a design guideline in which manifold inlet dynamic
pressure should remain smaller than the pressure drop across the
microchannels for uniform flow.

Why this matters:
This points toward a practical "reduced-order design tool" space.
Instead of requiring a full CFD solution for every geometry, one can
build a fast model for preliminary design and use CFD/experiment only
for the final candidates.

Another paper:
Kuiphuis, D.J.G. et al.
"Thermal-hydrodynamic modeling and design for microchannel cold plates
subjected to multiple heat sources."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 262, 125220, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.125220

Reported:
- straight, manifold, and serpentine architectures treated with a
  thermal-hydrodynamic model;
- thermal resistance and pressure drop predictions generally within
  10% under specified conditions;
- manifold prediction was less accurate and required tighter
  parameter restrictions;
- demonstrated use of the model for optimization without CFD in the
  inner design loop.

Interpretation:
The manifold remains a difficult modeling target because it couples
3-D redistribution, local channel flow, and thermal spreading.

======================================================================
5. RESEARCH AXIS C: TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZATION
======================================================================

Topology optimization (TO) is now a major cold-plate research category,
not a niche topic.

Why TO is attractive:
Instead of manually selecting a channel topology, material can be
redistributed inside a design domain to satisfy thermal and hydraulic
objectives.

Common formulations:
    minimize Tmax
    minimize Rth
    minimize pressure dissipation
    minimize weighted objective:
        J = w_T * thermal_metric + w_P * hydraulic_metric

Recent papers are moving from proof-of-concept to realistic constraints.

5.1. Multi-chip module with experimental validation

Ozguc, Serdar et al.
"Experimental investigation of an additively manufactured cold plate for
multi-chip module cooling generated using the homogenization approach to
topology optimization."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 258, 124741, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.124741

Problem:
- 8 separate heat sources
- off-center inlet/outlet
- flow-distribution challenge
- benchmark: uniformly distributed 1.2 mm pin fins

Reported:
- benchmark maximum base temperature rise: 35.5 °C
- topology-optimized design: 6.6 °C
- experimental pressure-drop MAPE: 5.5%
- experimental thermal-resistance MAPE: 9.7%

Important point:
The paper is valuable not simply because "TO improved performance",
but because it demonstrates the full pipeline:

    problem definition
       -> topology optimization
       -> manufacturing constraint
       -> additive manufacturing
       -> experiment
       -> numerical/experimental comparison.

5.2. Topology-optimized manifold microchannel

Zhou, Jianhong et al.
"Topologically optimized manifold microchannel heat sink with extreme
cooling performance for high heat flux cooling of electronics."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 241, 122426, 2024.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.122426

Reported:
- 1570 W/cm^2 heat dissipation
- 11.9 kPa pressure drop
- flow-rate mismatch among manifold inlets < 6%
- reported COP up to 78,273 at one operating point.
The architecture combines a manifold with topology-optimized
microchannels rather than optimizing a conventional parallel channel
layout.

Caution:
The very high heat-flux number is an embedded / high-flux experimental
benchmark and should not be compared directly with large-footprint
cold-plate studies without accounting for area, coolant temperature,
boiling regime, and boundary conditions.

5.3. TO under parameterized design conditions

"Effects of design parameters and inlet-outlet configuration on topology
optimization of cold plates for electronic cooling and numerical
investigations."
Case Studies in Thermal Engineering, 73, 106708, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.csite.2025.106708

Investigated:
- weighting factors
- inlet Reynolds number
- heat flux
- fluid volume fraction
- solid material
- inlet/outlet ratio, number, and position.

Reported:
- average temperature decreased with increased fluid volume fraction
  by up to 1.39 K in the tested cases;
- extended inlet/outlet structure performed well;
- TO designs showed 36-70% improvement over circular pin-fin benchmarks
  over the reported range.

Interpretation:
The "optimal topology" is not universal. It depends strongly on:
    boundary condition + heat flux + flow rate + material + objective weights.

This is a critical point for a paper claiming "optimized cold plate."

======================================================================
6. RESEARCH AXIS D: TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZATION IS NOW BEING PUSHED
   TOWARD REALISTIC ENGINEERING CONDITIONS
======================================================================

This is one of the clearest changes in 2026.

6.1. Engineering-oriented topology optimization + composite pin fins

Li, Baoyi et al.
"Engineering-oriented topology optimization of liquid-cooled cold plates
for high-power chips using topology-guided composite pin-fin reconstruction."
Applied Thermal Engineering, Article 133361, online 22 Sep 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.133361

Why this paper is important:
The authors explicitly criticize a common weakness of prior TO studies:
idealized low coolant temperatures, high flow rates, and lower heat fluxes
can make a design look excellent but less relevant to actual data-center
operation.

They impose practical constraints:
- coolant inlet temperature = 313.15 K (40 °C)
- allowable coolant temperature rise = 20 K
- flow-rate limit per unit power.

Then:
    TO for macro flow organization
        +
    reconstructed composite pin-fins for local convection.

Experiments:
- 1500 W heat load
- 1.0-2.5 L/min
- multiple TO-based cold plates.

Reported at 1.5 L/min:
- one design reduced maximum measured surface temperature by
  7.36 K and 5.18 K relative to skived-fin and pin-fin references,
  respectively;
- thermal resistance decreased by 18.5% and 13.9% relative to those
  references.

This is a strong example of the current shift:
    topology does the distribution problem,
    local fins do the convection problem,
    and the whole design is constrained by real operating conditions.

6.2. Topology optimization for arrays of discrete heat sources

Bairwa, Avinash; Weibel, Justin A.
"Performance enhancement offered by topology optimization in the design
of cold plates for cooling arrays of discrete heat sources."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 300, 131459, July 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.131459

Studied:
- device width
- device pitch
- number of devices.

Reported sensitivity:
The TO designs were especially sensitive to the number of devices in
the streamwise direction.

Physical interpretation:
TO is effectively routing coolant to each heat source instead of
treating the entire plate as a uniform heated surface.

This is highly relevant to:
    multi-die packages
    chiplet packages
    accelerator modules
    multi-chip power electronics.

======================================================================
7. RESEARCH AXIS E: NON-UNIFORM HEAT FLUX AND HOTSPOTS
======================================================================

This may be the most useful research-gap category for many conference
abstracts.

The conventional simplification is:
    q'' = constant

But real chips and multi-chip packages can have:
    q'' = q''(x,y,t)

Important variants:
- spatially non-uniform heat
- multiple discrete heat sources
- moving / intermittent hotspots
- transient thermal pulses.

7.1. Adaptive hotspot cooling

Li, Wenfei et al.
"CFD simulation of novel adaptive pin-fins microchannel heat sink to
improve thermal management of electronic chips."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 252, 123667, 2024.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.123667

Idea:
Fixed channel geometries are not ideal for hotspots that move in time
and space. The paper introduces adaptive pin-fin deformation and uses
dynamic-grid CFD to respond to hotspot conditions.

Interpretation:
This is conceptually different from static TO.
Static TO:
    design once -> operate at all conditions.

Adaptive cooling:
    structure or flow configuration changes with heat load.

This opens research into:
    thermal load sensing + active flow redistribution
    variable channel resistance
    adaptive pin-fin / manifold systems.

7.2. Non-uniform heat source + TO

Wu, Jing-peng et al.
"Topological optimization and thermal performance of cold plates for
lithium-ion battery with non-uniform heat sources."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 2024.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.123922

Reported:
- heat transfer coefficient improved by up to 26% versus conventional
  design at the same flow rate in the tested cases;
- maximum temperature deviation reduced by about 20%;
- numerical trends supported experimentally.

Although the application is battery thermal management, the methodological
lesson is directly relevant to electronics:
do not optimize against an artificial uniform wall heat flux if the
physical heat-generation map is known.

8. RESEARCH AXIS F: TPMS AND 3-D ARCHITECTED COLD PLATES
======================================================================

TPMS = triply periodic minimal surface.

Common structures:
- Diamond
- Gyroid
- I-WP.

Why TPMS is attractive:
- high surface-to-volume ratio
- continuous 3-D pathways
- secondary flow / mixing
- geometric freedom enabled by AM
- possibility of spatial grading.

Key paper:
Chen, Junyu et al.
"Numerical and experimental investigation of TPMS-structured cold plates
for electronic device cooling."
Applied Energy, 401, 126745, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.apenergy.2025.126745

Compared:
- serpentine
- Diamond
- Gyroid
- I-WP.

Studied:
- porosity
- unit cell size
- graded unit-cell size.

Reported:
- Diamond had the best overall thermo-hydraulic performance among the
  studied TPMS structures;
- a graded unit-cell-size design was proposed;
- under pumping power below 2 W, ultimate heat flux exceeded 256.9 W/cm^2;
- graded design improved overall performance by 3.58-6.24% over the
  uniform counterpart;
- maximum relative deviations between numerical and experiment were
  reported as 7.5% for temperature, 13.9% for pressure drop, and 5.44%
  for heat-transfer coefficient.

Important interpretation:
"Using TPMS" alone is no longer a complete novelty statement.
The newer question is:
    Which topology?
    Which porosity?
    Which cell size?
    Should cell size be graded?
    Should the topology respond to q''(x,y)?
    Can the structure be fabricated reliably?
    How much does the 3-D complexity cost hydraulically?

8.1. Nature-inspired graded TPMS

Zhang, Fengrui et al.
"Nature-inspired gradient triply periodic minimal surface cold plate:
Coral wave structural design for enhancing electronics cooling."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 257, 128252, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2025.128252

Contributions:
- coral-inspired wavy TPMS concept;
- 3-D printed AlSi10Mg and Cu prototypes;
- porosity and cell-size study;
- comparison of thermal-hydraulic behavior.

Research implication:
The field is moving from "canonical TPMS" toward:
    graded TPMS
    bio-inspired distortions
    hybrid TPMS
    multi-material / functionally distributed structures.

9. RESEARCH AXIS G: MANIFOLD + JET + MICROCHANNEL HYBRID DESIGNS
======================================================================

A major 2025 frontier is combining multiple transport mechanisms rather
than relying on a single fin/channel mechanism.

9.1. Jet-enhanced manifold microchannels

Wu, Zhihu et al.
"Jet-enhanced manifold microchannels for cooling electronics up to a
heat flux of 3,000 W cm^-2."
Nature Electronics 8, 810-817, 2025.
DOI: 10.1038/s41928-025-01449-4

Architecture:
    tapered manifold
        +
    microjet layer
        +
    sawtooth-wall microchannel layer.

Fabrication:
- structures etched into backside of silicon using MEMS technology.

Reported:
- up to 3000 W/cm^2
- pumping power = 0.9 W/cm^2 at that reported maximum
- single-phase water
- COP up to 13,000
- 1000 W/cm^2 at maximum chip temperature rise of 65 K.

This is important because it demonstrates that extreme heat-flux
performance can come from combining:
    flow distribution
    jet impingement
    boundary-layer disruption
rather than simply reducing channel hydraulic diameter.

9.2. Distributed inlet-outlet jet impingement cold plate

Samal, Sangram Kumar et al.
"Thermal management of data centers: Chip-scale cooling using novel
distributed inlet-outlet jet impingement liquid cold plate."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 2025, 126360.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2025.126360

Reported:
- TDP > 1000 W investigated
- thermal resistance around 0.019-0.0224 °C/W depending on test
  condition / metric definition;
- 19.8% pressure-drop reduction vs skived-fin benchmark in the reported
  comparison;
- about 64% less pumping power in one comparison;
- numerical + experimental validation.

Distinctive feature:
Distributed inlet/outlet nozzles are used to control local jet impingement,
and the design integrates the lid directly to the cold plate to remove a
TIM2 layer in the stated configuration.

Research implication:
TIM is increasingly treated as part of the thermal architecture rather
than an afterthought.

======================================================================
10. RESEARCH AXIS H: ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING IS SHIFTING FROM
    "CAN WE PRINT IT?" TO "CAN WE PRINT IT RELIABLY AT SCALE?"
======================================================================

AM enables:
- complex 3-D topology
- fine fins
- internal manifolds
- conformal pathways
- graded structures.

But AM introduces:
- minimum feature size
- roughness
- powder / support constraints
- dimensional deviation
- print failure
- internal passage blockage
- thermal conductivity differences from bulk material
- scale-up / throughput concerns.

10.1. TO + electrochemical additive manufacturing

Bazmi, Behnood et al.
"Ultra-high-performance cold plate development through topology
optimization and electrochemical additive manufacturing."
Cell Reports Physical Science, 7(5), 103272, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.xcrp.2026.103272

Reported:
- high-resolution pure-Cu cold plates
- up to 32% lower thermal resistance at fixed flow rate
- up to 68% lower pressure drop at equal thermal resistance vs pin-fin
  designs
- modular framework from mm-scale to cm-scale die footprints.

The key contribution is not merely TO:
    TO + a manufacturing process capable of realizing the small-scale
    architecture.

10.2. Metal AM wavy fins

Zaki, Omar M. et al.
"Metal additively manufactured wavy fin cold-plate architecture for
improved thermal-hydraulic performance."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 256, 128138, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2025.128138

Experiments:
- 1 kW heat dissipation
- 1-4 LPM water.

Reported:
- wavy fins reduced normalized thermal resistance by roughly 35-45%;
- at fixed 20 kPa pressure drop, variable-waviness lowered peak surface
  temperature by 9 K and thermal resistance by 51% in the reported
  comparison;
- edge-channel maldistribution limited gains for constant-amplitude
  waviness.

That last point is critical:
    a geometrically stronger local heat-transfer mechanism can be
    undermined by flow maldistribution.

10.3. Large-footprint scalable etching

Qiu, Haoyun et al.
"Scalably etched large footprint copper cold plates for high-power
dissipation in data centers using R1233zd(E)."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 256, 127938, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2025.127938

Architecture:
- 76 x 76 mm footprint
- 2-phase cooling
- R1233zd(E), a lower-GWP refrigerant
- chemically etched copper boiling surfaces.

Reported:
- heat load up to 2 kW
- saturation inlet temperature 71 °C
- etching improved HTC up to about 1.9x before dryout for one design
- case-to-coolant thermal resistance 4.9 K/kW at 2 kW
- negligible pressure-drop change attributed to the surface etching.

Engineering significance:
high coolant temperature can support facility-side energy benefits,
but only when chip-to-coolant resistance is kept sufficiently small.

======================================================================
11. RESEARCH AXIS I: 2026 PACKAGE-INTEGRATED COOLING
======================================================================

The cold plate itself is no longer always the final frontier.
Some groups are reducing the thermal gap by integrating channels into
the package.

11.1. Direct-to-package cooling

Martin, Henry A. et al.
"Co-packaged electronics with microfluidics for direct-to-package
cooling."
Communications Engineering 5, 92, 2026.
DOI: 10.1038/s44172-026-00620-9

Reported:
- ~625 W/cm^2 tested heat dissipation
- ~2-4 mL coolant volume in the demonstrated system
- ~6-7x lower junction temperatures and thermal resistances than
  ambient-air reference
- ~2-3x lower than heat-sink cooling in the reported comparisons
- effective global Nu > 10.

Key idea:
microchannels are embedded in a package substrate instead of requiring
microchannels directly in the semiconductor.

Why this matters to cold plates:
Traditional cold plate architecture has a thermal chain:

    chip
      -> TIM
      -> lid / spreader
      -> cold plate
      -> coolant

Package integration attempts to shorten the chain:

    chip / package
      -> integrated microchannel
      -> coolant.

Therefore a future "cold plate" research project may need to be positioned
against package-integrated solutions, especially when the study claims
very low thermal resistance.

11.2. Topology-optimized near-chip / direct-to-package cooling

"Topology-optimised near-chip microfluidic cooling for high power
electronics."
Case Studies in Thermal Engineering, 85, 108358, September 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.csite.2026.108358

Reported:
- topology optimization applied to a manufacturable co-packaged
  microfluidic substrate;
- peak temperature, temperature variance, and pressure drop used as
  targets;
- total thermal resistance reduction of 13.4% at 0.2 ml/s and 9.6% at
  0.1 ml/s;
- average pressure drop reduced by 55% over tested flow rates;
- experimental maximum power dissipation density 753.1 W/cm^2 over the
  heater area;
- peak COP about 13,923.

Interpretation:
The frontier is now "integrated thermal architecture optimization,"
not just "cold plate internal fin optimization."

======================================================================
12. RESEARCH AXIS J: MULTI-LAYER AND CROSS-FLOW MICROCHANNELS
======================================================================

Wang, Zi-Xing et al.
"Heat transfer and pressure drop characteristics of microchannel cold
plate in commercial CPU-package cooling system."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 246, 127060, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2025.127060

Application:
commercial Intel i9-9900K CPU package model.

Compared manifold configurations:
- Z
- U
- I.

Key result:
- I-type manifold was reported as better among the three studied;
- pressure drop decreased as microchannel layer number increased;
- a modified stepped multi-layer design was proposed;
- the improved configuration reached reported surface heat flux of
  206.5 W/cm^2.

Interpretation:
Multilayer designs can change both pressure distribution and flow
resistance, but layer stacking also raises fabrication and thermal
interface questions.

======================================================================
13. RESEARCH AXIS K: TWO-PHASE FLOW BOILING
======================================================================

Single-phase:
    Qdot ~ mdot * cp * DeltaT

Two-phase:
    phase change adds latent-heat transport.

Why it matters:
Two-phase cooling offers a potentially much larger effective heat
capacity rate and very high HTC.

But the penalty is complexity:
- flow instability
- pressure oscillation
- vapor distribution
- dryout
- critical heat flux
- surface condition sensitivity
- refrigerant compatibility.

13.1. Radial microchannels

"Flow boiling in novel radial microchannels for cooling of electronic
devices and modules of annular temperature distribution."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 239, 126555, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2024.126555

Reported:
- radial microchannel geometry
- 350 W/cm^2 heat flux at 3 g/s
- boiling heat transfer can reduce thermal resistance at low flow rates.

Key design logic:
radial flow can help reduce temperature maldistribution associated with
conventional straight parallel channels in annular heat sources.

13.2. Enhanced flow boiling for multi-chip

Cui, Chuanchuan et al.
"Experimental study of heat dissipation for high-power multi-chips using
enhanced microchannel flow boiling."
International Communications in Heat and Mass Transfer, 169, 109901,
2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2025.109901

Architecture:
- open microchannels
- sintered copper powder reinforcement
- both single cold plate and four-cold-plate parallel system
- electronic fluorinated liquid VN-NODCGIA1 (boiling point 47 °C).

Variables:
- inlet temperature
- mass flux
- non-uniform thermal loading.

Important:
This is an example of the field moving from single-device steady,
uniform tests toward multi-device systems and non-uniform thermal loads.

13.3. InterPACK 2025: direct-on-die two-phase cooling

ASME InterPACK 2025 paper:
"Direct on Die Two Phase Cooling Approach for High Power GPUs"
Paper 164278.

The abstract explicitly frames two-phase direct-to-chip cooling as a
response to rapidly rising GPU/CPU TDP, emphasizing:
- high HTC from boiling
- dielectric working fluids
- reduced leakage risk relative to conductive water on sensitive hardware.

Conference program places this in a dedicated "Two Phase Cooling" session.

14. RESEARCH AXIS L: TRANSIENT HEAT LOADS
======================================================================

A significant 2026 step is extending TO from steady heat loads to
time-dependent CHT.

Ji, Ritian et al.
"Topology optimization method of cold plates for transient conjugate
heat transfer process based on continuous adjoint method."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 259, 128387, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2026.128387

The work introduces:
- continuous-adjoint transient topology optimization
- transient conjugate heat transfer
- OpenFOAM finite-volume implementation
- time-varying heat sources.

Reported design outcome:
vein-like / biomimetic cold-plate structures emerged under time-varying
heat sources.

Research significance:
Most conventional cold-plate optimization is effectively "design for
one operating point." Real AI workloads can have changing power maps.
This creates a possible research direction:

    optimize over a heat-load trajectory, not a single q''.

Potential objective:
    minimize max_t Tmax(t)
    + lambda * DeltaP
    + beta * thermal non-uniformity.

15. RESEARCH AXIS M: SYSTEM-LEVEL DATA CENTER OPTIMIZATION
======================================================================

A cold plate can be thermally excellent but systemically inefficient.

At system level:
    pump power
    CDU
    facility water temperature
    heat exchanger approach temperature
    rack distribution
    control strategy
    fault tolerance
all matter.

15.1. Real-time optimization

"Real-time optimization of the liquid-cooled data center based on cold
plates under different ambient temperatures and thermal loads."
Applied Energy, 363, 123101, 2024.
DOI: 10.1016/j.apenergy.2024.123101

The study models data-center equipment and adjusts operating parameters
for different ambient conditions and thermal loads to reduce cooling
energy and improve PUE.

Research implication:
The cold plate is increasingly part of a control problem rather than
a fixed passive component.

15.2. Server-level coolant distribution

Khoshvaght-Aliabadi, M. et al.
"Impact of coolant distribution design on server-level thermal management
in data centers."
Energy, 330, 136936, 2025.
DOI: 10.1016/j.energy.2025.136936

Studied:
- two-CPU and four-CPU server layouts
- series / parallel coolant distribution
- finned vs smooth cold-plate deployment.

A reported hybrid arrangement reduced pumping power by about 10% while
keeping CPU temperature difference around 1 K.

Interpretation:
Even if two cold plates are identical, how they are connected can alter
the entire server-level thermal-hydraulic behavior.

15.3. 500-kW data-center testbed

Yan, Meiyue et al.
"Enhancing data centre energy efficiency and reliability through hybrid
cooling: a topology-optimized integrated cold plate approach."
Energy, 342, 139582, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.energy.2025.139582

Reported:
- topology-optimized hybrid liquid-air cold plate
- live 500 kW data-center testbed
- optimal flow around 1.25-1.5 L/(min·kW) in the study
- CPU temperature reduction by 5-7 °C
- air-only mode sustained 67% TDP in the stated setup as a fault-tolerant
  mode
- PUE reduction from 1.38 to 1.23
- reported 3.7% IT power saving and 50% pumping-power saving.

This is a strong example of the transition:
    component optimization -> system deployment.

16. RESEARCH AXIS N: RELIABILITY, CORROSION, EROSION, CLOGGING
======================================================================

This area is less crowded than thermal performance, which makes it
potentially useful for research differentiation.

A liquid loop introduces failure modes absent from conventional air
cooling:
- corrosion
- erosion
- clogging / fouling
- leakage
- coolant chemistry drift
- material incompatibility.

ITherm 2025:
"System level Reliability modeling of direct-to-chip liquid cooled data
centers."

The study uses FMEA and explicitly examines:
- erosion
- corrosion
- clogging.

It emphasizes that the reliability of the cold plate cannot be inferred
from thermal performance alone.

ASME InterPACK 2025:
"Effect of High Flowrates in a Liquid Cooled Direct-to-Chip Data Centers."
DOI: 10.1115/IPACK2025-164318

The study examines high-flow-rate erosion effects under a data-center-like
cooling loop. It investigates copper and EPDM under extended coolant
exposure and uses:
- SEM
- optical microscopy
- ICP-MS
- coolant pH
- conductivity
- turbidity
- inhibitor concentration
- copper / metal-ion traces
and monitors thermal resistance / pressure difference.

The paper is especially relevant to a practical trade-off:

    flow rate up
      -> heat transfer up
      -> pressure drop up
      -> potential erosion / durability concerns up.

Potential research gap:
Very few cold-plate papers optimize thermal-hydraulic performance and
long-term material reliability together.

17.1. Copper-tungsten

Pinkus, Ilan et al.
"Ultra-low thermal resistance and pressure drop copper and
copper-tungsten diamond-shaped pin fin cold plates for liquid cooling
of electronics."
International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer, 256, 128080, 2026.
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2025.128080

Architecture:
- 75 x 75 mm cold plate
- diamond-shaped pin fins
- copper / CuW / AlSi10Mg
- 300 candidate designs screened computationally.

Reported:
- diamond pin fins about 15% lower thermal resistance than conventional
  straight-fin microchannels
- best design:
    6.9 K/kW (Cu) at 1 kW
    9.0 K/kW (CuW) at 1 kW

Why CuW:
lower CTE than copper, potentially allowing tighter package integration
and reduced thermomechanical mismatch.

This is a useful reminder:
the optimal cold plate may be defined by:
    thermal + hydraulic + thermomechanical constraints.

======================================================================
18. RESEARCH AXIS P: FLOW DISTRIBUTION IS OFTEN THE HIDDEN LIMIT
======================================================================

Across multiple 2024-2026 papers, the same pattern appears:

    strong local heat-transfer mechanism
           +
    poor global flow distribution
           =
    less benefit than expected.

Examples:
- wavy fins: edge-channel maldistribution can limit performance;
- manifold systems: inlet/outlet configuration controls channel flow;
- multi-chip modules: off-center ports require deliberate routing;
- TPMS: cell size and porosity influence both resistance and local
  convection;
- TO: much of the value comes from routing fluid toward heated regions.

Thus, one useful research framework is to split performance into:

    MACRO scale:
        manifold / flow distribution / routing

    MESO scale:
        channel / fin / jet geometry

    MICRO scale:
        roughness / surface chemistry / boiling nucleation.

Many current papers improve one scale at a time.
A stronger research concept can intentionally couple two or three scales.

19. RESEARCH AXIS Q: REDUCED-ORDER MODELS, SURROGATES, AI/ML
======================================================================

The computational cost of detailed 3-D CFD becomes problematic when the
design space contains dozens or hundreds of variables.

Common approaches:
- response surface
- Kriging / Gaussian process
- ANN
- convolutional neural network
- surrogate-assisted multi-objective optimization
- Bayesian optimization
- genetic algorithm
- TOPSIS / MCDM.

Representative recent work:
"Topology optimization for cold plate using neural networks as proxy
models." Engineering Optimization, published online 2024.
DOI: 10.1080/0305215X.2024.2308555

The concept:
- use neural networks as surrogate / proxy models,
- optimize average cell temperature and pressure drop.

Battery-specific but methodological:
Ebbs-Picken, Takiah et al.
"Hierarchical thermal modeling and surrogate-model-based design
optimization framework for cold plates used in battery thermal management
systems."
Applied Thermal Engineering, 253, 123599, 2024.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.123599

The framework uses:
- hierarchical thermal modeling
- deep encoder-decoder neural networks
- full pressure / velocity / temperature field prediction
- manufacturability constraints.

Research implication:
A promising general architecture is:

    CFD / experiment
        -> dataset
        -> surrogate model
        -> optimization
        -> constrained geometry
        -> final CFD
        -> experiment.

The novelty should not be "we used AI."
It should be:
    AI enables a design problem that was otherwise too expensive.

20. HIGH-HEAT-FLUX BENCHMARKS: WHY NUMBERS ARE HARD TO COMPARE
======================================================================

The 2024-2026 literature contains dramatic numbers:
- ~206 W/cm^2 (commercial CPU-package style cold plate)
- ~256.9 W/cm^2 (TPMS cold plate)
- ~350 W/cm^2 (radial boiling microchannel)
- ~753.1 W/cm^2 (near-chip D2P microfluidic)
- ~1000 W class chip cooling
- 1570 W/cm^2 (TO manifold microchannel)
- 2250 W/cm^2 (staggered Z-type manifold microchannel)
- 3000 W/cm^2 (jet-enhanced manifold microchannel)
and some even larger embedded / boiling numbers.

These are NOT directly equivalent.

At minimum, normalize or report:
1. heated area
2. total heat input
3. local vs average heat flux
4. coolant type
5. inlet temperature / saturation temperature
6. flow rate and pressure drop
7. single-phase vs two-phase
8. thermal resistance definition
9. TIM included or excluded
10. maximum surface / junction temperature
11. test vs CFD
12. uncertainty.

A strong conference paper should avoid using "highest heat flux" as the
main claim unless the comparison basis is carefully controlled.

21. WHAT THE 2024-2026 LITERATURE SUGGESTS ARE THE REAL RESEARCH GAPS
======================================================================

Gap 1. Realistic heat maps
--------------------------
Many studies still use uniform heat flux.
Modern electronics increasingly demand:
    q''(x,y,t).

Opportunity:
    cold-plate design conditioned on an actual chip thermal map.

Gap 2. Flow distribution + hotspot coupling
--------------------------------------------
A geometry can have high local HTC but poor coolant allocation.
Opportunity:
    optimize coolant routing and local heat transfer simultaneously.

Gap 3. "Optimal" design under realistic water temperature
----------------------------------------------------------
TO studies can become overly aggressive when cold coolant and unlimited
flow are allowed.
Opportunity:
    impose realistic facility-water inlet temperatures, coolant
    temperature rise, flow-per-kW, and pumping-power constraints.

Gap 4. Manufacturability-aware optimization
--------------------------------------------
A mathematically optimal geometry may be impossible to fabricate or may
print poorly.
Opportunity:
    include minimum feature size, surface roughness, overhang,
    dimensional tolerance, and post-processing in the optimization.

Gap 5. AM-induced performance uncertainty
-----------------------------------------
Need:
    as-designed geometry
    vs as-built geometry
    vs measured thermal/hydraulic response.

Gap 6. Thermal performance + reliability
-----------------------------------------
Few studies integrate:
    Rth + DeltaP + corrosion + erosion + clogging + leakage risk.

Gap 7. Transient workloads
--------------------------
Most steady-state optimization assumes one operating point.
Opportunity:
    optimize over a heat-load trajectory / duty cycle.

Gap 8. Package / TIM / cold-plate co-design
-------------------------------------------
Cold plates are increasingly integrated closer to the die/package.
Opportunity:
    co-optimize TIM thickness, spreader, base plate, and microchannels.

Gap 9. Component-to-system scaling
-----------------------------------
Many papers stop at the cold-plate test loop.
Opportunity:
    connect cold plate behavior to:
        CDU -> rack -> heat rejection -> PUE.

Gap 10. Experimental visualization
----------------------------------
Reviews still highlight the lack of internal flow visualization and
validated flow-pattern models for complex microchannels.
Opportunity:
    high-speed imaging, transparent prototypes, IR thermography,
    local pressure measurements, synchronized flow/temperature data.

24. IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: THERMAL PERFORMANCE VS SYSTEM PERFORMANCE
======================================================================

A common mistake is to report:

    "Tmax decreased 10 K"

and conclude the design is better.

Suppose the price is:

    DeltaP x3
    flow rate x2
    pump power x6.

Then the thermal improvement may not translate to system-level
improvement.

A better experimental design is to compare at:
- same flow rate
- same pressure drop
- same pumping power
- same inlet temperature.

For a fair thermo-hydraulic comparison, choose the constraint that
matches your engineering question.

26. WHAT TO AVOID IN A COLD-PLATE ABSTRACT
======================================================================

Avoid:
1. "A novel cold plate was designed."
   Too generic.

2. "Heat transfer was enhanced by increasing the surface area."
   Mechanism is generic.

3. "The proposed structure has better performance."
   Define the performance metric.

4. Reporting only Tmax.
   Include pressure drop / pumping power.

5. Comparing heat flux values across papers without matching conditions.

6. Calling a design "optimal" without defining the objective function,
   constraints, and design space.

7. Saying "additive manufacturing solves the fabrication problem."
   AM introduces a different set of constraints and uncertainty.

30. HYPOTHESIS SPACE FOR A FUTURE CONFERENCE ABSTRACT
======================================================================

The following are not claims about what is already established; they are
candidate research hypotheses suggested by the literature.

H1:
A cold plate optimized using a realistic 2-D heat map will reduce
temperature non-uniformity more efficiently than a geometry optimized
under uniform heat flux.

H2:
For multi-chip modules, reducing channel-to-channel flow variation can
produce a larger benefit in Tmax than increasing local heat-transfer
coefficient at constant total flow.

H3:
A two-level architecture that separately optimizes macro coolant routing
and local convective enhancement can outperform a monolithic topology
optimization under the same pumping-power limit.

H4:
Manufacturability-aware topology optimization can produce lower
experimental performance scatter than unconstrained topology
optimization, even if the ideal CFD solution is slightly less optimal.

H5:
At elevated facility-water temperature, low chip-to-coolant thermal
resistance becomes more important than maximizing nominal heat-transfer
coefficient.

H6:
For pulsed AI workloads, a transiently optimized cold plate can reduce
peak temperature without requiring the same steady-state flow rate as
a steady-state-optimized design.

H7:
Thermal-hydraulic optimization that ignores erosion/corrosion may select
flow conditions that are unfavorable over long operating periods.

32. BOTTOM-LINE RESEARCH POSITIONING
======================================================================

The literature does NOT suggest that "cold plates are solved."

Rather, it suggests that the simple channel-geometry problem is becoming
increasingly mature, while more realistic coupling problems remain open.

The most credible emerging problems are:

    realistic heat map
          +
    flow distribution
          +
    hydraulic constraint
          +
    manufacturability
          +
    package / TIM interaction
          +
    transient operation
          +
    reliability
          +
    system-level energy.

    Real chip heat map
          |
          v
    Hotspot-aware cold plate
          |
          +---- manifold / flow distribution
          |
          +---- topology / TPMS
          |
          +---- local fin / jet enhancement
          |
          +---- manufacturability constraint
          |
          v
    CFD + experiment
          |
          v

    WHAT realistic problem was imposed?

    WHY does the result matter at system level?

    external cold plate

        -> package-integrated cooling

    CFD-based parametric optimization

        -> robust / multi-objective optimization

34.1. NEW TREND: THE COOLING BOUNDARY IS MOVING INWARD
----------------------------------------------------------------------

One of the clearest additions from the 2026 literature is a shift from
asking "what cold plate geometry should be used?" to asking "where should
the coolant be allowed to interact with the thermal stack?"

A conventional package-level thermal path can be represented as:

    junction
      -> die / die attach
      -> package substrate / lid
      -> TIM
      -> cold plate
      -> coolant.

As heat density increases, reducing convective resistance inside the cold
plate eventually gives diminishing returns if conduction and interface
resistance upstream remain dominant.

The current progression is therefore:

    package-integrated
      -> direct-to-package (D2P)
      -> interposer-embedded
      -> direct-to-die / near-junction
      -> in-chip microfluidics.

A 2026 review proposes an especially useful interpretation: once the
cooling boundary is moved inward, the dominant bottleneck can migrate
from package-side conduction to hotspot spreading, interfacial thermal
resistance, local coolant allocation, and phase-change stability. This is
important because a paper reporting a very high heat flux does not
necessarily solve the same thermal bottleneck as a large-footprint cold
plate.

Reference [A1]:
Wei, J., Lin, S., Fu, J., Zhao, Z., Wei, N. (2026).
"Microfluidic cooling for high-heat-flux chips: Thermal-path compression,
bottleneck migration and near-junction limits."
Thermo-X 2, 202623.
DOI: 10.70401/tx.2026.0024
URL: https://doi.org/10.70401/tx.2026.0024

Research implication:
For literature comparison, it is more rigorous to classify a cooling
architecture by the resistance segment that it actually removes. This
prevents a near-junction 1-mm hotspot experiment and a 75-mm cold plate
from being treated as interchangeable demonstrations simply because both
report W/cm^2.

34.2. NEW TREND: 2.5D / 3D INTERPOSER AND CHIPLET-LEVEL COOLING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The original report already mentions package-integrated and D2P cooling,
but the additional 2026 literature shows a more specific branch:
interposer-level liquid cooling for heterogeneous integration.

Why this matters:

    chiplet density increases
        -> lateral heat spreading paths become coupled
        -> neighboring chiplets thermally interact
        -> a single global cold plate cannot independently control each
           die temperature.

Therefore, cooling architecture and chiplet layout can no longer always
be treated independently.

A. TSV-interposer embedded microfluidic cooling

Gong, Yufan et al. (2026) designed and fabricated a TSV interposer with
embedded microfluidic cooling for 2.5D packaging. The study explicitly
uses non-uniform power distributions and examines micro-pin-fin geometry,
arrangement, pressure drop, and temperature uniformity. Square pin fins in
staggered arrangement achieved the highest reported PEC among the studied
configurations, 1.57. The paper also emphasizes that Cu TSVs perform two
roles simultaneously: electrical interconnection and thermal spreading.

The important research lesson is not simply that "staggered fins work."
Rather:

    electrical interconnect geometry
        + thermal spreading
        + coolant routing
        + non-uniform die power
        = one coupled package problem.

Reference [A2]:
Gong, Y., Xu, S., Zhang, Y., Li, Q., Huang, H., Chen, X. (2026).
"Flow and heat transfer characteristics of embedded microfluidic cooling
in TSV interposer for 2.5D packaging."
International Communications in Heat and Mass Transfer 173, 110866.
DOI: 10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2026.110866
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2026.110866

B. Heterogeneous 2.5D integration and thermal crosstalk

Lu, Guoran et al. (2026) developed a realistic 2.5D thermal test vehicle
with EMC and RDL and investigated embedded manifold microchannel cooling.
The reported system handled 948 W total power at approximately 400 W/cm^2
while targeting suppression of inter-chiplet thermal crosstalk. An RC
network model was used to analyze the thermal interaction between chiplets.

This is a particularly important development because it expands the
meaning of "temperature uniformity." It is not sufficient to minimize the
maximum temperature of each die independently; the thermal response of one
die can influence the temperature of its neighbors.

Reference [A3]:
Lu, G. et al. (2026).
"Synergistic thermal management of heterogeneous 2.5D integration:
Embedded manifold microchannel cooling for high-flux heat dissipation and
thermal crosstalk suppression."
Applied Thermal Engineering 300, 131418.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.131418
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.131418

C. Buried hotspots in 3D GaN chiplet stacks

Jin, Yuanyuan et al. (2026) examined embedded microchannels in a 3D GaN
power chiplet architecture for automotive inverters. The key idea is that
some heat sources become physically buried inside a 3D stack and cannot
be effectively cooled by a conventional top-side cold plate.

The reported architecture places coolant within sub-10-um proximity of
the thermal source. The study reports up to approximately 100 C reduction
in peak junction temperature and a 65% reduction in thermal resistance in
the investigated configurations.

This supports a fundamental design principle:

    When the hotspot is buried,
    improving the external cold plate may be less effective than moving
    the coolant boundary closer to the hotspot.

Reference [A4]:
Jin, Y., Huang, L., Zheng, Y., Li, Y., Song, T. (2026).
"Embedded micro-channel liquid cooling for thermal management of 3D GaN
power chiplet systems in automotive inverters."
Applied Thermal Engineering 303, 132223.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.132223
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.132223

D. Near-substrate chiplet cooling before 2026

Wu, Yongjin et al. (2025) proposed a Tesla-like rotor-wing microchannel
near-substrate heat sink specifically for chiplets. The optimized sample
was experimentally fabricated and reported a heat-dissipation capability
of approximately 1446 W/cm^2.

The important point for trend mapping is that chiplet cooling is not only
a 2026 phenomenon; 2025 work was already moving the cooling structure
closer to the substrate and into the package-level design space.

Reference [A5]:
Wu, Y. et al. (2025).

Applied Thermal Engineering 258, 124775.

34.3. NEW TREND: HOTSPOT COOLING IS BECOMING A PACKAGE-ARCHITECTURE PROBLEM

The additional literature strengthens the original report's observation
that q'' should be represented as q''(x,y), and potentially q''(x,y,t).
However, an important further development is that hotspot management is
being coupled to package geometry rather than treated only as a boundary
condition on a cold plate.

2025 chiplet microsystem work used multiple hot spots within a 2.5D package
and assessed rib filling mode and arrangement using entropy generation and
field-synergy analysis. The study is useful because it explicitly treats
multiple hot spots rather than one uniform heater.

A complementary 2026 paper on asymmetric heat sources goes one step
further by coupling multi-objective topology optimization with a
conditional diffusion-model digital twin. The model generates new cold
plate geometries conditioned on physical requirements and then uses a
surrogate model for rapid performance screening.

Reference [A7]:
Wu, H., Coaty, J., Zong, Y., Azar, K., Minary-Jolandan, M., Tian, Z., Xu, Y.

This is a stronger formulation than manually choosing a single channel

34.4. NEW TREND: COLD PLATE + THERMAL SPREADER + TRANSIENT BUFFER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Another additional direction is hybridization of the cold plate with
thermal spreading or thermal buffering structures.

A 2026 composite liquid cooling plate combines a micro-pin-finned vapor
chamber with liquid cooling. The reported maximum heat load was 350 W,
corresponding to approximately 242.9 W/cm^2, with a thermal resistance of
0.059 K/W. Relative to the conventional liquid-cooling comparison used in
the study, the maximum heat flux increased by 78.2% and thermal resistance
decreased by 62.9%.

The significance is structural:

    liquid convection
        removes heat continuously

    vapor chamber
        redistributes heat laterally before it enters the liquid path.

This is particularly relevant to localized hot spots, because the thermal
resistance from a small hot region to a channel may contain a spreading
component that cannot be removed by simply increasing the local HTC.

Reference [A8]:
Wang, J. et al. (2026).
"Composite liquid cooling plate based on a micro-pin-finned vapor chamber
for chip cooling in high heat flux applications."
Applied Thermal Engineering 302, 131916.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.131916
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.131916

A separate 2026 study integrates a topology-optimized liquid cold plate
with metal-skeleton-enhanced phase-change material (PCM) for transient
heat-shock mitigation. The reported peak-temperature reduction relative
to the topology-optimized cold plate alone was approximately 50-67 K for
1500-2100 W transient heat loads.

This establishes a useful new branch:

    steady-state cold plate
        -> cold plate + transient thermal buffer
        -> cold plate optimized for workload dynamics.

Reference [A9]:
Liu, Z. et al. (2026).
"Design and investigation of a topology-optimized liquid cold plate
integrated with metal-skeleton-enhanced phase change material for
transient heat shock mitigation."
Applied Thermal Engineering, Article 133012.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.133012
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.133012

Research implication:
For AI workloads with bursty power, the relevant question can be

    "What is the maximum transient temperature excursion?"

rather than only

    "What is the steady-state thermal resistance?"

34.5. NEW TREND: PRACTICAL TWO-PHASE COLD PLATES ARE MOVING TOWARD
      LOW-FLOW, PARALLEL, AND DYNAMIC OPERATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The original report already covers boiling, but the 2026 literature gives
a clearer picture of what makes two-phase cooling practically interesting:
not simply achieving a high critical heat flux (CHF), but maintaining
stable operation at low flow rates and under varying thermal loads.

A. Tapered-gap two-phase cold plate

Choi, Hwang and Lee (2026) fabricated a tapered-gap two-phase cold plate
to enhance CHF under low-flow conditions. The study reports a minimum
thermal resistance of 0.061 K/W, heat flux above 187.5 W/cm^2, and pressure
drop near 1 kPa for one investigated geometry. CHF increased with
inclination angle, which the authors relate to bubble growth and departure
behavior.

Reference [A10]:
Choi, J., Hwang, S., Lee, J. (2026).
"Thermal-hydraulic performance of tapered gap two-phase cold plate for
thermal management in high-power electronics."
Thermal Science and Engineering Progress 75, 104762.
DOI: 10.1016/j.tsep.2026.104762
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsep.2026.104762

B. Eight-cold-plate parallel two-phase GPU system

Zheng et al. (2026) developed a pump-driven parallel system containing
eight cold plates. The study sustained a maximum chip heat load of
2300 W, approximately 300 W/cm^2, and explicitly reports stable
operation under dynamic and non-uniform loads.

This is important because it attacks one of the key barriers to two-phase
cooling scale-up: how multiple parallel evaporative paths share flow and
remain stable as heat load moves between devices.

Reference [A11]:
Zheng, C., Jiang, Q., Ge, Z., Ge, Y., Chen, D. (2026).
"Heat dissipation limit and scenario adaptability of parallel two-phase
cold plates for high-power GPUs."
Applied Thermal Engineering 294, 130621.
DOI: 10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.130621
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2026.130621

Research implication:
A useful research question is increasingly:

    how to maintain stable phase distribution among multiple cold plates
    under non-uniform, time-varying chip power?

This is much closer to a rack-scale engineering problem than a single-
channel CHF experiment.

C. Controlled bubble injection

A 2026 study investigated controlled bubble injection into microchannel
cold plates for high-power chips, explicitly motivated by the limitation
of conventional single-phase cooling at high TDP. Bubble-assisted transport
belongs to an intermediate research space between single-phase enhancement
and fully developed boiling operation.

Reference [A12]:
Afridi, M.I. et al. (2026).
"Enhancing the thermal performance of microchannel cold plates via
controlled bubble injection for next-generation high-power electronic
chips."
International Communications in Heat and Mass Transfer 176/associated
2026 article record, DOI: 10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2026.110621.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icheatmasstransfer.2026.110621

Note:
The publication record should be checked directly against the final
journal issue before using volume/page information in a manuscript.
The research direction itself is clear: controlled gas/liquid interaction
is being explored to enhance transport without relying solely on a

34.19. UPDATED RESEARCH-GAP MAP AFTER THE ADDITIONAL SEARCH
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The original document identified ten important gaps. After incorporating
the additional 2025-2026 literature, those gaps can be refined as follows.

Gap A — Actual spatial heat maps remain underused
-------------------------------------------------
Non-uniform and asymmetric heat sources are increasingly studied, but many
cold-plate optimization studies still use uniform heat-flux assumptions.
The stronger research question is:

    Can the cold plate be designed directly from a realistic q''(x,y)
    extracted from an actual die, chiplet, or workload map?

Gap B — Hotspot routing and local HTC are not always optimized together
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Several papers show that high local heat-transfer enhancement can be
undermined by poor flow distribution. A two-level design remains attractive:

    macro routing / manifold
             +
    meso local enhancement
             +
    hotspot-specific heat map.

Gap C — Thermal crosstalk between neighboring chips is underexplored
---------------------------------------------------------------------
For chiplet and 2.5D modules, temperature of Chip A depends on Chip B's
power and spacing. A useful metric is not only Tmax but also a thermal
coupling matrix or cross-sensitivity:

    S_ij = dT_i / dQ_j.

A cold-plate study that minimizes both self-heating and cross-heating could
be significantly more package-relevant.

Gap D — Warm-water optimization needs more coupled experiments
---------------------------------------------------------------
High inlet temperatures expose the real facility-level energy problem.
More studies should report:

    Tin = 40-45+ C
    coolant temperature rise
    flow per kW
    pumping power
    chip/junction temperature
    Rth.

This is a stronger practical benchmark than room-temperature water alone.

Gap E — As-built geometry uncertainty is still not standard
------------------------------------------------------------
A mathematically optimal geometry is not necessarily the experimentally
optimal geometry. Future work should compare:

    as-designed
    vs as-built
    vs measured.

This can be formalized as robust optimization rather than an after-the-fact
correction.

Gap F — Long-term reliability remains less mature than thermal optimization
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Thermal-hydraulic optimization commonly operates over minutes or hours.
Practical systems need:

    months / years
    + erosion
    + corrosion
    + fouling
    + leakage
    + coolant aging.

The research gap is therefore a life-cycle thermo-hydraulic problem.

Gap G — Transient optimization is becoming important
-----------------------------------------------------
2026 work now directly optimizes transient CHT, and PCM-assisted cold plates
are emerging. This makes duty-cycle-aware optimization a credible frontier.

Gap H — The package stack must be part of cold-plate optimization
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Future co-design should include:

    die
    + die attach
    + interposer
    + TIM
    + spreader / vapor chamber
    + cold plate.

A cold plate optimized with an artificial zero-TIM boundary can otherwise
misrepresent the real junction temperature.

Gap I — System-level pumping-power allocation is under-coupled to package design
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The cold plate receives only a finite share of the rack's hydraulic budget.
A geometry that is optimal in isolation may be suboptimal when manifold,
CDU and rack losses are included.

Gap J — Experimental flow visualization remains a major bottleneck
------------------------------------------------------------------
The 2025 Chemical Engineering Science review explicitly highlights the
need for better in-situ flow visualization and reliable flow-pattern
prediction in microchannels. This creates room for:

    transparent cold plates
    high-speed imaging
    IR thermography
    local pressure sensing
    synchronized flow and temperature measurements.

Gap K — Benchmarking standards are still incomplete
----------------------------------------------------
Papers commonly report heat flux, but definitions vary:

    heater area
    die area
    active area
    cold-plate footprint.

Similarly, thermal resistance may use:

    junction-to-coolant
    surface-to-coolant
    base-to-coolant
    case-to-coolant.

A useful contribution could be a common benchmark protocol rather than a
new geometry.

Gap L — Reliability / serviceability / sustainability should be co-designed
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
OCP and LCA literature show that coolant, materials, serviceability and
system energy matter. A future multi-objective formulation could include:

    thermal
    + hydraulic
    + manufacturability
    + reliability
    + sustainability.

    pumping power = DeltaP x Vdot
    flow non-uniformity
    pressure distribution.

3) Heat-load definition

    total Q
    average q''
    peak local q''
    heated area
    heat-map q''(x,y)
    transient q''(x,y,t) when applicable.

4) Boundary definition

    Tin
    Tsat if boiling
    coolant chemistry
    pressure
    inlet/outlet arrangement
    TIM thickness / conductivity
    package stack.

5) Manufacturing

    process
    material
    minimum feature size
    surface roughness
    as-built dimensional error
    post-processing
    internal blockage / powder removal.

6) Reliability

    corrosion indicators
    erosion indicators
    coolant pH / conductivity where relevant
    particle concentration / turbidity
    pressure drift
    Rth drift over time
    leakage / containment record.

7) System relevance

    flow per kW
    pump efficiency assumption
    CDU boundary
    facility water temperature
    rack-level coolant distribution
    PUE or cooling overhead where applicable.

8) Uncertainty

    sensor accuracy
    flow-meter accuracy
    heater power uncertainty
    temperature uncertainty
    pressure uncertainty
    propagation of uncertainty.

34.22. FAIR COMPARISON RULES FOR HIGH-HEAT-FLUX COLD PLATES
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The additional records reinforce the warning in the original report that
heat-flux numbers alone are easy to misinterpret.

A strong comparison should align at least:

    same or explicitly normalized heated area
    same total heat load or heat flux definition
    same coolant
    same inlet temperature
    same thermal boundary
    same pressure-drop definition
    same fixed-flow / fixed-DeltaP / fixed-pumping-power basis
    same thermal-resistance definition
    experimental vs numerical status.

For high-heat-flux studies, also state:

    hotspot dimensions
    background heat flux
    maximum allowable temperature
    whether the device is in a package, interposer, or bare substrate
    whether the coolant is single-phase or two-phase.

A 73.5 kW/cm^2 hotspot on a tiny active region is a different engineering
problem from a 73.5 kW/cm^2 average over a 50-75 mm cold-plate footprint.

34.27. KEY CONCEPTUAL SYNTHESIS: FROM "COLD PLATE OPTIMIZATION" TO
       "THERMAL ARCHITECTURE CO-DESIGN"
----------------------------------------------------------------------

After the additional 2026 literature search, the field can be summarized
more precisely as a progression in the location and definition of the
design problem.

Stage 1 — Geometry optimization

    channel width / height / pitch / fin geometry.

Stage 2 — Flow-distribution optimization

    manifold / inlet / outlet / parallel-branch resistance.

Stage 3 — Heat-load-aware optimization

    multiple dies / hotspots / q''(x,y).

Stage 4 — Topology optimization

    free-form coolant routing under thermal + hydraulic objectives.

Stage 5 — Manufacturing-aware topology

    minimum feature size / AM roughness / as-built deviations.

Stage 6 — Package-integrated cooling

    D2P / interposer / near-substrate architectures.

Stage 7 — Near-junction cooling

    coolant boundary moved closer to the source.

Stage 8 — Multi-functional thermal architecture

    cooling + electrical + mechanical package functions.

Stage 9 — Dynamic / autonomous optimization

    transient CHT + surrogate + digital twin + generative AI.

Stage 10 — System deployment

    CDU + rack + facility water + PUE + sustainability + reliability.

The resulting research question becomes:

    "How should the thermal architecture of a high-power electronic
     system be co-designed so that a realistic heat-load map can be
     removed through a manufacturable and reliable liquid path under a
     finite hydraulic and facility-energy budget?"

This is broader and technically more defensible than:

    "Which cold-plate channel shape provides the highest heat-transfer
     coefficient?"

34.28. PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY FOR A CONFERENCE PAPER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

If the intended project is a conventional 40-60 mm-class water-cooled cold
plate rather than an in-chip microfluidic device, the most defensible
research niche is likely to be built around one or more of the following
physical constraints:

    realistic non-uniform heat map
    + flow distribution
    + warm coolant inlet
    + pumping-power limit
    + manufacturability
    + experimental validation.

The reason is not that extreme embedded cooling is unimportant. Quite the
opposite: the literature shows that it is rapidly advancing. The challenge
is that record heat-flux demonstrations often operate at a much smaller
scale and with much tighter device integration than a conventional cold
plate. A conventional cold plate can therefore be differentiated by making
its realism explicit.

A useful conceptual architecture for such a study is:

    Realistic chip heat map
            |
            v
    Thermal spreading model
            |
            v
    Flow-distribution-aware manifold
            |
            v
    Hotspot-targeted local enhancement
            |
            v
    Manufacturing constraint
            |
            v
    CFD / surrogate optimization
            |
            v
    AM / machining / brazing
            |
            v
    Experimental validation
            |
            v
    Rth + Tmax + uniformity + DeltaP + pumping power
            |
            v
    Warm-water / rack-level relevance.

This architecture directly connects many of the individual trends found
across the 2024-2026 literature without requiring the project itself to
become an in-chip microfluidics fabrication project.

34.30. FINAL SYNTHESIS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The original reconnaissance already established that the cold-plate field
is moving from channel geometry toward coupled thermal-hydraulic design.
The additional 2026 search sharpens that conclusion:

1. The bottleneck is migrating inward.
   Package/TIM resistance is increasingly replaced by hotspot spreading,
   interfacial resistance, local coolant allocation and two-phase stability.

2. Chiplet and 2.5D/3D packages are changing the problem geometry.
   A single global cold plate cannot always independently solve multiple
   interacting heat sources.

3. Flow distribution remains a hidden limiter.
   More aggressive local fins/TPMS/jets do not guarantee better system
   performance when coolant allocation is poor.

4. Warm-water operation changes the optimization objective.
   Facility-side energy and heat rejection can be as important as chip
   temperature.

5. Manufacturing is part of the physics.
   Roughness, minimum feature size and as-built deviation can materially
   change the thermal-hydraulic response.

6. Two-phase cooling is moving toward system realism.
   Parallel evaporators, dynamic loading and stable flow distribution are
   becoming more important than isolated CHF records.

7. AI is changing the design loop.
   Surrogates, Bayesian optimization, diffusion models, transfer learning
   and physics-informed search are being used to reduce the cost of
   geometry exploration.

8. Reliability and sustainability are moving closer to the core problem.
   OCP standardization and LCA studies make coolant compatibility,
   serviceability, energy and water use relevant research variables.

Accordingly, the most research-ready formulation is no longer:

    "Design a better cold plate."

It is:

    "Design a manufacturable liquid-cooling architecture that delivers
     coolant according to a realistic thermal-load map, maintains thermal
     and temperature-uniformity targets under a finite hydraulic budget,
     and remains compatible with package, coolant, reliability and
     system-level constraints."

This formulation can still be implemented as a conventional cold plate.
The novelty comes from the physical problem definition and the coupling
of constraints, not necessarily from using an exotic microchannel shape.
