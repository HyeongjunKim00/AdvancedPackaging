## Abstract

The growing demand for data processing requires higher memory capacity and bandwidth from artificial intelligence (AI) accelerators. High bandwidth memory (HBM) improves electrical performance through additional die stacking and higher data rates, while increased thermal resistance within the stack limits further scaling. To further enhance the memory capacity and bandwidth while mitigating thermal challenges, vertically oriented memory architectures have recently emerged [1], [2]. In a vertically oriented dies (V-die), each die conducts heat in-plane to a top-mounted cold plate, avoiding the series thermal resistance of horizontal HBM die stacking. However, the heat must travel along the die height and exit only through the narrow top surface of each thin die, which limits further increases in power density and stacking density. In this work, we propose an inter-die liquid cooling scheme for the V-die. To preserve inter-die spacing during wafer-level assembly, Cu pillars are formed on the passivated DRAM wafers and bonded to the backside of the neighboring wafers. The resulting gaps serve as coolant passages, allowing liquid to remove heat directly from the die faces and limiting heat accumulation within the V-die. The exposed pillar surfaces provide additional area for heat transfer to the coolant (**Figure 1(a)**). We first compare top-side cold-plate cooling with the inter-die liquid cooling architecture in two scenarios: the die count is varied from 10 to 40 in steps of 5 at a fixed power per die, and the die pitch is reduced within a fixed planar footprint to increase stacking density. The inter-die liquid-cooling architecture is further investigated to quantify the effects of coolant type, flow rate, and Cu-pillar arrangement on the maximum die temperature (*T*max) and pressure drop (Δ*p*). For each combination of coolant and flow rate, configurations of pillar-free, in-line, and staggered Cu-pillar with pitches ranging from 250 µm to 5 mm are compared, while the pillar diameter and height are fixed at 100 µm. A test vehicle with passivated silicon dies and bonded Cu-pillar spacers is fabricated to assess structural feasibility. *T*max and Δ*p* are measured using infrared thermography and inlet-outlet pressure measurements, respectively, under coolant flow and compared with the corresponding simulation results. Finally, comprehensive thermal-hydraulic design maps are generated to delineate the geometric windows for effective heat removal. The results demonstrate that the Cu-pillar inter-die liquid cooling method reduces *T*max by up to [X%-] and thermal crosstalk by [Y °C] compared with a conventional top-mounted cold plate scheme, providing the thermal headroom required to increase vertical die stacking within a fixed planar footprint.
