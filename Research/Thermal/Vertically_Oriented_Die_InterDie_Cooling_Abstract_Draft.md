## Working title

**Thermal–Hydraulic Design of Copper-Pillar Inter-Die Cooling for Vertically Oriented Memory-Die Stacks**

## Abstract

The throughput of AI accelerators depends on the memory capacity and bandwidth available to the processor. Vertically oriented DRAM stacks can increase memory capacity and edge-accessible I/O density within a compact package footprint. However, vertical orientation places die sidewalls face to face across narrow gaps, creating a confined region where coolant access and heat transfer must be designed together. A top-mounted cold plate removes heat from an exposed package surface. It does not directly supply coolant to the opposing vertical die surfaces. Conventional top-side cooling therefore leaves the inter-die heat-removal path undefined for vertically oriented memory stacks.

In this work, we evaluate single-phase direct liquid cooling through the gaps between vertically oriented DRAM dies. Copper pillars span adjacent die surfaces and act as heat-transfer fins: their exposed surfaces add wetted area, while solid conduction transfers heat from the dies to the pillar surfaces, from which heat convects to the coolant. However, each pillar contacts both dies and can also conduct heat from one die to the other. The pillars may therefore improve coolant-side heat transfer while increasing thermal coupling between the memory dies. The design question is whether pillar geometry can lower the hottest die temperature without imposing an excessive pressure drop or transferring heat toward a cooler die.

The study defines thermal and hydraulic criteria for inter-die cooling of vertically oriented DRAM stacks. The coupled analysis distinguishes a pillar’s role as a coolant-exposed fin from its role as a solid thermal bridge between dies. The findings can guide selection of pillar spacing and dimensions for single-phase cooling architectures and provide boundary conditions for later package-level analysis with the processor and external cold plate.
