## Abstract

The growing demand for data processing requires higher memory capacity and bandwidth from artificial intelligence (AI) accelerators. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) increases DRAM capacity through die stacking, whereas the routing area available for TSV-based I/O constrains further bandwidth scaling. As additional dies and bonding layers are stacked, heat must conduct through the stack, increasing thermal resistance and limiting further scaling [1], [2]. To address these coupled scaling constraints, vertically oriented die (V-die) architectures have been proposed [3], [4]. Upright DRAM dies use side-edge pads for I/O; adding dies can therefore increase memory capacity and the number of I/O connections, supporting higher bandwidth. Silicon’s high in-plane thermal conductivity (*k* = 140 W/m·K) provides a heat-spreading path from each die toward a top-mounted cold plate, avoiding the through-stack heat path across multiple dies and bonding layers. However, the long in-plane path to the narrow upper die edge and thermal coupling between adjacent dies may still limit die power and count.

An inter-die liquid-cooling architecture is proposed to provide direct coolant access to the broad faces of vertically oriented DRAM dies. During wafer-level assembly, Cu pillars mechanically support neighboring dies and maintain gaps for coolant flow. The pillars are thermocompression-bonded to Cu landing pads on the backside of the overlying DRAM wafer, providing a solid heat-conduction path between dies. After dicing and vertical mounting on an interposer, the gaps between adjacent upright dies form coolant passages, while the exposed pillar surfaces add coolant-wetted heat-transfer area. The coolant-facing DRAM surfaces and exposed Cu-pillar sidewalls are conformally passivated to electrically isolate them from the coolant, while the Cu-to-Cu bond interfaces remain uncoated (**Figure 1(a)**). This bonding and selective-passivation sequence is a proposed process route and remains to be validated for the target geometry.

To benchmark the cooling concept at an HBM4-representative die count and isolate the Cu-pillar contribution, simulations first evaluate a 12-die horizontal HBM reference with top-side cold-plate cooling and a 12-die V-die stack with inter-die liquid cooling.
